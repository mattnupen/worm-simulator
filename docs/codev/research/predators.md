# Research: animals that eat earthworms (predators topic)

Checked 2026-10-11 for the in-game lesson "Who eats worms?" (`src/content/lessons/predators.ts`,
process in `docs/codev/in-game-lessons.md`). Each page was opened, and the quote is the sentence
that supports the fact. A few quotes came through a summarising fetch, so re-check exact wording on
the live page before class. **Used** marks the facts the lesson shows.

## Watch out
- **The "worm grunting" study (Catania 2008) did not use nightcrawlers.** It used a native Florida
  worm (*Diplocardia mississippiensis*) and the eastern mole. No source shows nightcrawlers coming
  up to escape moles. Their sourced escape is the reverse: pulling back down the burrow. A game
  where nightcrawlers flee up must label it "Game choice".
- **Salamanders win and lose.** Worm Watch says salamanders "may be both negatively and positively
  affected". Keep the "may".

## Could not source well (leave out, or label "Game choice")
- Nightcrawlers coming up to escape moles (only shown for the Florida worm).
- The star-nosed mole's share of earthworms in its diet (much of it is aquatic prey).
- The robin's earthworm percentage.
- Leeches and centipedes as Minnesota earthworm predators.
- Mucus or bad taste as a defence.
- Worms "feeling light through their skin" (the behaviour, pulling back from light, is sourced; the sense organ isn't).
- "A baby robin eats 14 feet of earthworms" (an EPA flyer, with no study behind it).
