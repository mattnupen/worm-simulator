# Research: nightcrawler anatomy (*Lumbricus terrestris*) for the 3D model

Checked 2026-10-23 for building a scientifically accurate 3D nightcrawler (outside and inside) and
for the in-game anatomy lessons. Segments are counted from the front, with the mouth segment
(peristomium) as segment 1. The prostomium is the small lobe in front of it and is not counted. A
septum "9/10" means the wall between segments 9 and 10.

Each page was opened, and the quote is the sentence that supports the item. Quotes came through a
summarising web reader, so check the exact wording on the live page before a quote goes on a
slide or into `src/content/`. Two of the strongest sources are old (Smith 1917, Rolleston and
Jackson 1888), but their anatomy still matches modern sources. Confidence: **high** means a
species-level or university source states it plainly and nothing disagrees; **medium** means one
source, a genus- or family-level source, or a small disagreement; **low** means a weak source
or a real disagreement.

## Watch out

- **Crop and gizzard positions vary by source.** Clarendon College puts the crop in 15–16.
  Rolleston and Jackson (1888, *L. terrestris*) put it in 16–17, with the gizzard in 17–18. Fox
  (Lander University) says the esophagus "joins the … crop in about segment 12", which looks too
  far forward. Model the **crop in about 15–16 and the gizzard in about 17–18**, with the
  intestine starting at about 19. In real worms the organs are soft and shift by a segment, so a
  one-segment spread is normal.
- **Pharynx and esophagus.** Fox says buccal cavity 1–3, pharynx 3–5 and esophagus 6–12.
  Rolleston and Jackson say the pharynx fills "the first five somites" and the esophagus runs
  "through ten somites" (about 6–15). Clarendon says esophagus 6–13. A school dissection page says
  pharynx 1–6. Use **pharynx about 2–5 and esophagus about 6–14**.
- **Heart segments.** Wikipedia (*Earthworm*) says the aortic arches are in segments 7–11, and
  Wikipedia (*Lateral heart*) says 6–11 for *L. terrestris*. Fox and Science Learning Hub give
  **five pairs** but no segments. Fox also says "ten segmental hearts", which is the same thing
  (5 pairs = 10). Use **5 pairs, in 7–11** (medium confidence).
- **First dorsal pore.** Smith (1917, a species key) puts it at **7/8**. Fox says "all segments
  posterior to 12" have a pore, which would put the first one at about 12/13. Trust the key
  (Smith) for the model. The pores are tiny and don't need to be modelled.
- **Seminal vesicles.** Smith says the sperm sacs are "in 9, 11, and 12" (three pairs), and so
  does Clarendon College. Fox says the three pairs are "lateral outpockets of two … sperm
  reservoirs in segments 10 and 11" and does not put a segment on each pair. These agree:
  the vesicles grow out from the reservoirs in 10 and 11 into 9, 11 and 12.
- **Clitellum.** Smith says 32–37, "sometimes beginning on 31". British Annelids and GVSU say
  32–37. Clarendon's "segments 30–40" is loose. A school dissection page says "33 to 37" and also
  puts the male pores "on about segment 26", which is wrong: **don't use that page**.
- **Total segments.** Smith gives 110–180, Wikipedia *L. terrestris* "around 120–170, often
  135–150", and Wikipedia *Lumbricus* "around 110–160". Use about **140–150** for the model.
- **Length.** Wikipedia gives 110–200 mm, UMN Extension 5–8 inches, Smith 10–30 cm and Colorado
  State 20–25 cm. NatureSpot says the biggest reach 35 cm *when stretched while moving*. A
  resting adult is about 15–20 cm long and 7–10 mm wide.
- **Setae spacing.** Smith says closely paired behind the clitellum, and Wikipedia
  (*Lumbricus*) says "widely paired at both ends of the body". Both are true: the pairs open out
  at the head and tail ends.
- **Spektrum (German encyclopedia) says "8 paarige Borsten"** ("8 paired setae") per segment.
  That means 8 setae in 4 pairs, not 16. Every other source agrees on eight per segment.
- **No source was found for:** the segment where the typhlosole starts; which segment is the first
  with nephridia (Wikipedia: none in "the first three"); setae size in mm; how thick each body
  wall layer is; whether a septum sits between segments 1 and 2. Leave these out of lessons, or
  record them as game choices if the model needs them.

**Bristle close-up (Virtual worm, 2026-10-24): recorded choices.** Never shown on screen as facts:
- The drawn bristle is 0.95 mm long and slides 0.5 mm out, because no measured size was found. The 1 mm bar on screen is true to the body (8 mm wide, from the width row above).
- Only the belly pair (a, b) is drawn, at the cut. The side pair is left out so the close-up stays simple.
- Squeeze and stretch amounts, wave length (7 segments) and speed are chosen for clarity, not measured.
- The pocket and its three muscles are simplified strands.
- Round 2 (Matt, 2026-10-24): the squeeze is one smooth wave along the body, and grip is shared by every place whose bristles are out. Soil grains (sizes chosen, not measured) are pushed straight outward out of the body's way and stay there; the head's taper over five segments and the lobe's lift are drawn, not measured.
