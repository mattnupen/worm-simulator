# Research: how earthworms eat (V4 topic: eating and getting energy)

Checked 2026-10-21 for the in-game lesson "How do worms eat?" (`src/content/lessons/eating.ts`,
process in `docs/codev/in-game-lessons.md`). Matt set the topic on 2026-10-21: eating and getting
energy, with some worm anatomy. The hook is that worms must come up to the top leaf layer for food,
which is where predators like the robin hunt. Each page was opened, and the quote is the sentence
that supports the fact. Quotes came through a summarising web reader, so re-check exact wording on
the live page before class. **Used** marks the facts the lesson shows.

## Watch out
- **The leaf order is for nightcrawlers.** Worm Watch ranks basswood, then sugar maple, then oak
  for *Lumbricus terrestris*. The lesson says "nightcrawlers", not "worms".
- **"Energy" isn't sourced as a worm fact.** No source here says how a worm turns food into energy
  or how much it eats a day. The lesson stays with what is sourced: what worms eat, how the food
  moves through the body, and how much of the leaf layer they can eat. An energy meter in the game
  would be a **Game choice**.
- **Worms don't eat living roots or green plants.** They eat dead and decaying matter, plus the
  fungi and bacteria on it. A student design where the worm eats living plants would be a game
  choice (recorded, never stated as a fact in the game).
- **The eating film simplifies.** Inside, the worm is drawn much bigger than life, and food goes
  through in about 30 seconds (really it takes hours). No measured mouth size was found, so none is
  stated. These are recorded here, not labelled in the game (Matt, 2026-10-23).

## Could not source well (leave out, or keep as a game choice)
- How much a worm eats a day ("its own weight a day" is common online, with no study behind it here).
- How a worm turns food into energy, or how long it can go without food.
- Microbes "softening" leaves before nightcrawlers eat them (widely said; not found on a checked page).
- Worms "smelling" or "tasting" food (the sense organs aren't sourced here).

## Likely student myths
| Myth | What's true | Fact # |
|---|---|---|
| Worms chew with teeth | No teeth; the gizzard grinds with sand | 1 |
| Worms eat dirt and nothing else | Some kinds swallow soil for the dead bits and microbes in it; nightcrawlers eat fallen leaves | 16, 19 |
| Worms eat living plants and roots | They eat dead and decaying matter, and fungi | 12 |
| Worms eat any leaf | Nightcrawlers are picky: basswood first, oak last, needles barely | 13, 15 |

## The worm's insides (the 3D eating film, Matt 2026-10-23; first a drawing, 2026-10-22)
The "Follow the leaf" film's cutaway worm (`src/eatingFilm/inside.ts`) follows two school
dissection guides. Where nightcrawlers eat (Matt asked us to double-check, 2026-10-23): they come
out on damp nights and pull fresh surface litter down into the top of the burrow; leaf stems,
bits of leaf and casts pile up round the opening, the midden (Great Lakes Worm Watch, *Earthworm
Ecological Groups*; Colorado State, *Nightcrawlers*). The tail stays in the burrow, gripping its
sides (NatureSpot, *Common Earthworm*; shown, not stated). Worms that live and eat inside the leaf
layer are a different group, the leaf-litter worms.

| What the drawing shows | Source | Supporting quote |
|---|---|---|
| Prostomium over the mouth at the front | Home Science Tools, *Earthworm Dissection Guide* | "the fleshy bump over its mouth, called the prostomium" |
| Pharynx just inside the mouth | Home Science Tools | "This is the light-colored organ just inside the mouth." |
| Five hearts around the esophagus, behind the pharynx (used as a fact) | Home Science Tools; Carolina, *Earthworm Dissection* | "Behind the pharynx are five dark loops wrapped around the esophagus." / "the 5 pairs of aortic arches, or hearts, which circle the esophagus" |
| Crop, then gizzard | Home Science Tools | "Food from the esophagus is temporarily stored in the crop." "Food comes from the crop into the gizzard, where it is ground up." |
| One long intestine to the anus (used as a fact) | Home Science Tools | "The intestine is the long tube extending from the gizzard all the way to the anus." |
| Dorsal blood vessel over the gut, nerve cord along the bottom | Home Science Tools | "a dark line extending from the hearts over the top of the crop"; "the long white nerve cord running along the length of the worm" |
| Brain (cerebral ganglia) and the nerve cord from it | Carolina | "Identify the cerebral ganglia and the ventral nerve cord that extends from them" |
| Septa between segments (used as a fact) | Carolina | "Identify the thin walls between each segment. These are called septa." |
| Clitellum about a third of the way back | Carolina; Home Science Tools | "The clitellum is the enlarged structure that begins at segment 31." / "About one-third of the way back from the mouth" |
| Food soaking from the intestine into the body, sand in the gizzard, casts out the back | Science Learning Hub; U.S. Botanic Garden | facts 1, 3, 6 and 7 above |

Simplified on purpose: 100 segments (real nightcrawlers have more), the crop and gizzard placed by
the usual textbook layout (not sourced to a segment number), and the dots that rise from the
intestine to the blood vessel stand for food being taken in.
